## How many Adidas proxies do you actually need?

There is no useful universal formula, because the answer depends on concurrent activity, not on how exciting a release looks on social media.

A better way to estimate your requirement is to map the workflow first.

### Start with simultaneous sessions, not total tasks

If 10 legitimate sessions are active at the same moment and each needs a separate stable connection, begin by considering 10 IPs—not 50, 100, or 254. The issue is that HypeProxies’ public entry tier begins at 50 IPs, so your purchase decision must account for that minimum.

Do not assume one IP can safely serve unlimited simultaneous actions just because a provider advertises unlimited threads. “Unlimited threads” describes a provider-side allowance; it does not mean a retailer will accept an unlimited amount of activity from one IP.

### Keep the session coherent

Use one assigned static IP consistently for a single session where continuity matters. Switching geographies or identities during a queue, account login, or checkout flow is a bad operational habit and can cause the retailer to invalidate the session.

The goal should be normal, stable behavior—not an unrealistic volume of activity.

### Include room for testing and replacement

If you operate an allowed monitoring workflow, do not allocate every IP to production traffic from day one. Reserve a portion for connectivity checks and for isolating an address that performs poorly on a specific public endpoint.

That does not mean aggressively cycling through IPs to defeat a website’s controls. It means applying ordinary infrastructure hygiene: validate configuration, monitor errors, and reduce traffic when a service indicates that you are exceeding acceptable limits.

## Common mistakes with Adidas proxy setups

### Treating IPs as a guarantee

A static residential ISP IP can reduce one source of friction: IP reputation and session instability. It cannot guarantee inventory, queue placement, account approval, or checkout completion. Any provider promising otherwise is selling confidence more aggressively than infrastructure.

### Choosing rotating IPs for a persistent session

Rotating traffic can be useful for certain permitted data-collection jobs, but it is usually a poor fit when a workflow relies on continuity. If the task requires the same identity from page one through page five, use a stable assignment and avoid changing it halfway through.

### Ignoring total cost

A $1.06 effective per-IP rate on the Enterprise tier looks attractive until you remember it requires a quarterly commitment equivalent to $270 per month. Compare total cost, minimum purchase, billing period, and your actual concurrency—not only the smallest number in the pricing column.

### Using free public proxy lists

Free lists often contain slow, recycled, compromised, or already-blocked addresses. They can also create a security risk because you do not know who operates the intermediary server. For any legitimate business or research task, that is a poor trade-off.

### Forgetting that browser and account behavior still matter

A proxy is one component of a broader connection profile. Sudden location changes, inconsistent browser settings, unusual request patterns, and repeated failed payments can all create problems regardless of the IP quality. Keep workflows consistent, authorized, and within the platform’s rules.
